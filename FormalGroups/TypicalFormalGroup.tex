\section{$p$-Typical Formal Group Law}\label{sec:formalgroups-typicalFormalGroup}

    We continue to work over $\Spec(\Z_{(p)})$ here.

    Let $F$ be a formal group law over a $p$-local algebra $A$. Suppose $n$ is a positive integer coprime to $p$, then the $n$-series $[n]_Ft:=t+_Ft+_F...+_F t$ ($n$ times) is an invertible formal power series, and we let $[1/n]_F t$ denote the inverse. Let $\zeta$ be a $n$th primitive root of unity, then there is power series
    \[
    f_n(t):=[\frac{1}{n}]_F (t+_F \zeta t+_F \zeta^2 t+_F...+_F \zeta^{n-1}t)
    \]This is actually a power series over $A$ (since the formal group law is associative and symmetric, all coefficients are symmetric polynomial of $\zeta$, which lives in $\Z_{(p)}$).
\begin{definition}
    A formal group law $F$ is \textbf{$p$-typical formal group law} if for all prime $q\neq p$, $f_q(t)=0$. A homomorphism of $p$-typical formal group law is just a morphism of formal group law.
\end{definition}


\begin{remark}
    Suppose $F$ is a $p$-typical formal group law, then there exists element $v_i \in A$ with $p=v_0$ such that 
    \[
    [p]_F t=pt+_F v_1t^{p}+_F v_2t^{p^2}+_F...+_F v_nt^{p^n}+_F...
    \]
    Moreover, the element $v_i$ determines the $p$-typical formal group law uniquely.

    Notice, the height of $F$ is determined by the vanishing of $v_i$ entirely.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}
    Suppose $A$ is torsion-free $p$-local algebra, then the logarithm $\log_F(t)$ of the formal group law $F$ satisfies
    \[
    \log_F(t)= \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}m_{n}t^{p^n}
    \]for $m_{n}\in A\otimes\mathbb{Q}$ if and only if $F$ is $p$-typical.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    Suppose $\log_F (t)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_i t^i$ over $A\otimes\mathbb{Q}$.

    Let $q\neq p$ be a prime, and let $\zeta$ be a primitive $q$th root of unity, then by definition
    \[
    0=t+_F \zeta t+_F \zeta^2t+_F ...+_F \zeta^{q-1}t
    \]
    Then 
    \begin{align*}
        0=\sum_{j=0}^{q-1}\log_F(\zeta^{j}t)
        \\=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_i (\sum_{j=0}^{q-1}\zeta^{ji})t^i
    \end{align*}
    The primitive root of unity satisfies the property
    \begin{align*}
        \sum_{j=0}^{q-1}\zeta^{ji}=\begin{cases}
            q, \text{ if $q|i$}
            \\ 0, \text{ else}
        \end{cases}
    \end{align*}
    Since the equation above is true for all $q\neq p$, the equality implies that 
    \[
    \log_F(t)= \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{p^n}t^{p^n}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}m_{n}t^{p^n}
    \]

    For the other direction, we can use the same identity of primitive roots to conclude the $p$-typicalness.
\end{proof}

Taking one step further, we have
\begin{lemma}
    Suppose $A$ is torsion-free $p$-local algebra, then a formal group law $F$ is a $p$-typical formal group law if and only if 
    \[
    [p]_F t=pt+_F v_1t^{p}+_F v_2t^{p^2}+_F+...+_F v_nt^{p^n}+_F...
    \]

    Moreover, the coefficient $v_n$ determines the $p$-typical formal group law entirely.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    Then equation is equivalent to 
    \[
    p\log_F t=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\log_F(v_nt^{p^n})
    \]
    Since $A$ is torsion-free, then $p$ is invertible in $A\otimes \mathbb{Q}$, then 
    \[
    \log_F t=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}m_{n}t^{p^n}
    \\=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{p}\log_F(v_nt^{p^n})
    \]
    Comparing terms conclude the argument
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}
    Every $p$-local formal group law is isomorphic to a $p$-typical formal group law.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
    The proof requires the construction of a parametrization, that is an isomorphism of formal group law $\varphi(t)$ from our formal group law to a $p$-typical formal group law. Cartier provided a construction, such that the parametrization is idempotent. We will denote the parametrization of $F$ to be $T(F)$, and the isomorphism to be $\varphi_F: F\to T(F)$.

    The $p$-localized universal formal group law $F_{Univ}$ over $L_{(p)}$ is classified by a ring map $f: L_{(p)}\to L_{(p)}$. Note, $f^2=f$, because the parametrization is idempotent. 

    Of course, $L_{(p)}$ is torsion free, $G:=T(F_{Univ})$ is determined entirely by 
    \[
    [p]_G t=\sum_{n\geq 0}^G v_nt^{p^n}
    \]
    Recall how we get the Lazard's ring, which is determined by the logarithm coefficient. Using similar logic, the image of $f$ is 
    \[
        \Z_{(p)}[v_1,v_2,...]
    \]with the $p$-typical formal group law $G$.

    Now, every $p$-typical formal group law is determined by the classifying map $L_{(p)}\to A$, which by taking the parametrization, becomes a map
    \[  \Z_{(p)}[v_1,v_2,...]\to A
    \]determining the $p$-series.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}
    There exists a formal group law $G$ over $V_p:=\Z_{(p)}[v_1,v_2,...]$, we refer to it as the \textbf{universal $p$-typical formal group law}, such that 
    \[
    [p]_Gt=pt+_Gv_1t^p+_Gv_2t^{p^2}+_G...
    \]such that every $p$-typical formal group law $F$ is determined uniquely by a map $\phi:V_p\to A$ such that $\phi$ factors through $V_p/I_n$ if $F$ is of height at least $n$, with the coefficient $v_i^F$ determined as $\phi(v_i)$.
\end{theorem}

The Hopf algebroid $(V_p, V_p\otimes_L LB[u^{\pm}]\otimes_L V_p)$, with the induced coaction determines a stack
\[
[\Spec(V_p)/\Spec(V_p\otimes_L LB[u^{\pm}]\otimes_L V_p)]\to \Mfg\otimes \Spec(\Z_{(p)})
\]
The following result shouldn't be too hard to believe 
\begin{proposition}
There is equivalence $[\Spec(V_p)/\Spec(V_p\otimes_L LB[u^{\pm}]\otimes_L V_p)]\simeq \Mfg\otimes \Spec(\Z_{(p)})$
\end{proposition}

    The closed immersion $\Spec(V_p/I_n)\to \Spec(V_p/I_n)$ with the induced Hopf algebroid determines the formal group laws of height at least $n$, and there is the following.
\begin{corollary}
    The inclusion $\Mfg^{\geq n}\to \Mfg\otimes \Spec(\Z_{(p)}$ is a closed immersion.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}
    We will be working often with $p$-typical formal group law. We can use the methods of $p$-typical formal group laws for the study of chromatic homotopy theory and making things nice. 
\end{remark}
